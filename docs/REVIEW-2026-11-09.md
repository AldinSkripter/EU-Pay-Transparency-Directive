# Technical report review — 9 November 2026

Scope: payroll normalization, Article 9 indicators, Article 10 assessment contents, snapshot-bound management confirmation, recipient exports and the existing application integration suite.

Primary legal reference: [Directive (EU) 2023/970](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32023L0970), particularly Articles 3, 4, 7–10 and 12. The seven reporting indicators and the joint assessment have different contents; other provisions include employer processes and Member State responsibilities rather than additional fields in every report.

## Corrections

- Payroll numeric validation rejects whitespace-only values, arrays, objects and booleans rather than coercing them into payroll amounts. Explicit numeric zero remains valid for pay amounts.
- Report confirmation, internal review fields and assessment evidence are read only when their workflow snapshot ID matches the exported payroll snapshot. An old approval, justification or leave-return record cannot be presented as current evidence by the report serializers.
- The separately maintained employer control register remains included in the internal document, including its existing stale-state labels. It does not replace payroll management confirmation.

## Verification and limits

The final suite passed 204 tests with no failures and one external PostgreSQL integration test skipped because its database was not configured (205 total). Added regression coverage checks invalid payroll inputs and mismatched workflow snapshots. Production build succeeds; Vite reports the existing bundle-size warning.

The suite exercises numeric references, annual and hourly values, all seven indicators, the 5% boundary, the six-month assessment condition, historical reporting, recipient privacy, country/language selection, imports, approval invalidation, report formats, account flows and employer inputs. Passing these tests is technical evidence, not an independent legal certification or proof that every possible payroll input is correct.

National submission channels and official schemas remain unverified. The newly identified Upper Austrian public-sector amendment requires a separate applicability review; it must not be applied to every Austrian private employer. No new national legal regime is enabled by this review. Actual SMTP delivery, an external PostgreSQL deployment and real employer pilot reconciliation remain separate acceptance work.
